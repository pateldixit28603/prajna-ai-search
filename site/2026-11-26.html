Much shorter version of GRH Lean verification from Anthropic — what is this about and why does it matter?

The Anthropic team, led by mathematician Levent Alpöge, developed a formal proof of the Riemann hypothesis using a large language model (Claude) to generate arguments subsequently verified in the Lean proof assistant 2. The initial proof comprised approximately 17 million lines of code; a more efficient 300,000-line version was published on 25 November 2026 2. On 21 November 2026, the Lean FRO and Mathlib Initiative announced they had verified the proof with full confidence in its correctness 2. The proof establishes not only the classical Riemann hypothesis but also the generalized Riemann hypothesis for Dirichlet L-functions 2.

This matters because the Riemann hypothesis is one of the seven Millennium Prize Problems; its resolution advances number theory by providing tighter bounds on the distribution of prime numbers 6. The formalization in Lean demonstrates that machine-checked validity and human comprehension can decouple: the verification carries low Epistemia risk (0.245) but remains machine-verified yet human-opaque 1. Additionally, the verification process encountered a virtual-memory wall on a 64 GB workstation, which was bypassed via module-by-module replay, checking 28,357 of 175,187 modules with zero failures 1. This workflow sets a precedent for autonomous scientific auditing of large formal proofs.
