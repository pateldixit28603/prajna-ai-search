## Who or what is Moon, and what are the key facts to know?

Moon is Earth's only natural satellite, orbiting at an average distance of 378,000 km and completing a lunar month every 29.5 days [1]. It is tidally locked to Earth, always showing the same near side, and its gravity drives Earth's tides [1]. The Moon has a diameter of 3,474 km and mass of 1.2% of Earth's [1]. It appears in phases from new to full as it orbits Earth [3]. The first crewed landing was Apollo 11 in 1969, with six Apollo missions landing between 1969–1972 [4][7]. A 2009 sci-fi film titled *Moon* stars Sam Rockwell as a miner on the far side of the Moon [2]. Blue moons—second full moons in a calendar month—occur roughly every 2–3 years [5].

## Matrix Multiplication is Θ(n^2.25) — what is this about and why does it matter?

**Why it matters:**
- **Practical impact:** Faster matrix multiplication accelerates countless applications—from deep learning model training and scientific simulations to graphics rendering and graph algorithms. Even a small reduction in the exponent translates to massive speedups on large matrices.
- **Theoretical significance:** The "optimal" exponent ω (omega) is a major open problem in theoretical computer science. The current best bound (as of 2025) is O(n^2.371339) [2], meaning ω ≈ 2.371339. A result of Θ(n^2.25) would represent a breakthrough, improving upon the best-known asymptotic bound.
- **Algorithmic progress:** Over decades, researchers have discovered increasingly efficient algorithms. Strassen's algorithm (1969) was the first to beat n³, reducing the exponent to ~2.807. Since then, methods based on algebraic geometry, tensor decompositions, and group theory have incrementally lowered ω [3][4]. A Θ(n^2.25) algorithm would be a substantial leap forward.

**Current state of knowledge:**
- The exact asymptotic complexity of matrix multiplication remains unknown [1].
- The best proven upper bound as of September 2025 is O(n^2.371339) [2].
- Claims of proofs or new algorithms (such as the November 2026 OpenAI update mentioned in [1]) are subject to rigorous verification.

In summary, Θ(n^2.25) describes a hypothetical or newly proposed algorithm that would multiply matrices faster than any previously published method, with profound implications for both theory and practice. However, as of the latest reliable sources, the best established bound remains above 2.37.

The "spray bottle" matters because it bridges functional manipulation (robotics), personal care, agriculture, and medicine—requiring precise control of flow, pressure, and direction.
